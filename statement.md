## Problem Statement

In daily life, most people are busy with studies, work and social media, and they rarely stop to think about how their life is going. Serious tools like surveys or counselling forms can feel long, boring or heavy for a young person who only wants a quick, light check.

There is a need for a simple and fun tool that:

- asks a few easy personal and professional questions,
- takes only a minute to finish,
- and gives a friendly result with a short motivational message.

Life Checker solves this problem with a small Python console game. The player answers 15 Yes/No questions, and the program turns the answers into a score and shows one life result with a message. The game is only for fun and is not a real prediction.

## Scope of the Project

**Included in the project:**

- A console (terminal) game written in Python
- 15 positive Yes/No questions about personal life, habits and goals
- Questions asked in a new random order every time
- Checking of the player's input (Yes, No, y, n, capital letters, extra spaces); wrong input is counted as No
- Score calculation in percent
- 9 different life results, each with its own short message

**Not included in the project:**

- No graphical screen or website
- No saving of scores or player data
- No login or user accounts
- No real, scientific or medical prediction; the results are only for fun and motivation

## Target Users

- Students who want a quick and fun self-check
- Beginners learning Python, who can read the project as a simple example of lists, functions, loops and if-else
- Anyone who likes light personality-style games

## High-Level Features

- 15 Yes/No questions on personal life and habits
- Random order of questions in every game
- Easy answers: `Yes`, `No`, `y`, `n` in any letter case
- Error handling: wrong input is taken as No and the player is told
- Automatic score in percent
- 9 life results, from "perfect life" to "much bad case", with a motivational message for each
- Runs in any terminal with Python 3, with no extra library to install
